3. Reading: At School Abroad

A. Divya (went to Chile):
I went to a talk given by students who had been on an exchange programme. It occurred to me that if I could persuade my mum, this would be just the sort of break from normal school routine that I needed! Soon I was immersed in a completely different educational culture, which helped me appreciate many aspects of my school back home. Another great advantage was that I picked up Spanish quickly.

B. Nelson (went to New Zealand):
I lived on a farm with a vast flock of sheep. Being a city boy, the experience was totally novel. We was totally reliant on each other. I also looked forward to going to school every day. I took part in lots of sporting activities and made lifelong friends. I'm not so dependent now on my family or teachers to make me study, and I learned to get on with all sorts of people.

C. Bruce (went to England):
My parents insisted that I went, knowing what a sacrifice they were making to pay for it. What I found hardest to get used to were the gloomy days of grey drizzle and early darkness in winter. Even so, I took every chance to meet people and soak up the English life. I worked hard to get good grades, although I found the schoolwork challenging.

4. Grammar: Zero, 1st & 2nd Conditionals + Unless

Grammar Breakdown: Conditionals in Exam Tasks

• Zero Conditional: Facts & universal truths → If + Present Simple, Present Simple
If you heat water to 100°C, it boils.


• 1st Conditional: Real future situations → If + Present Simple, will / can / might + V1
If your tutor has to cancel a tutorial, he will let you know beforehand.


• 2nd Conditional: Imaginary / improbable situations → If + Past Simple, would / could + V1
If I lived in Italy, I would learn how Italians live and think.


• Unless = If not:
You cannot join the club unless you pay the fee (= if you do not pay).
