3. Reading: Lucy’s First Job

Paragraph 1. When I was just 16, my father bought an old guesthouse in our village and decided to turn it into a luxury hotel. At the early stages, he experimented with almost everything. None of our family had ever worked in a hotel before, but my dad had a clear vision of what guests would expect. His standards were uncompromisingly high, and he strongly believed that the key to achieving them was sheer hard work.

Paragraph 2. My main role was working as a waitress in the hotel restaurant. Carrying heavy trays between the hot kitchen and the elegant dining room was physically demanding. The chef had a terrifying ability to lose his temper, so I always grabbed the dishes with an anxious expression, which I had to transform into a warm, welcoming smile the moment I walked out to the guests.

Paragraph 3. To make my role more interesting, my father gave me a special task: preparing packed lunches for guests going on day trips and running the "Sweet Trolley" — a cart with freshly baked cakes and puddings. I experimented with traditional recipes and created new desserts. It was a wonderful opportunity to show creativity and receive direct feedback from the visitors.

Paragraph 4. Working there taught me a lot about human behavior. Breakfast guests were usually relaxed and friendly, whereas evening diners were dressed up, talked in louder voices, and were much more demanding. Although the job was exhausting and poorly paid, looking back, I realize it gave me self-confidence and practical skills that proved invaluable in my adult life.

4. Grammar: Countable & Uncountable Nouns in Work Context

Grammar Breakdown: Job vs. Work & Uncountable Traps

• Job [Countable]: Refers to a specific role, occupation, or profession. Takes a / an, can be plural.
She applied for a job as a receptionist. / He has had three different jobs.

• Work [Uncountable]: Refers to effort, employment, or general activity. Never takes a or plural -s.
He is looking for work in the city. (NOT: ~~a work~~, ~~works~~)

• Key EGE Uncountables: information, advice, accommodation, equipment, news, furniture, luggage.
Use partitives to express single units: a piece of advice / information / news / equipment.
